Research

  • High-z galaxies; Dusty star-forming galaxies; Galaxy Clustering; COSMOS; Lensing

In the area of observational astrophysics and galaxy evolution, my research is focused on two major topics: (1) clusters of galaxies in the early Universe, i.e., galaxy protoclusters and groups, (2) dusty star-forming galaxies, aiming to reveal their formation, growth, and evolution across the history of the Universe. I'm facinated by the most distant dusty galaxies and protoclusters, which are spectacular systems, and ideal laboratories to pioneer our view of galaxy and massive structure formation. Recently, I expand my field to gravitational lensing and quiescent galaxies at high redshifts.

For my research, I use multi-wavelength data ranging from optical to radio. I specialise in infrared (JWST, HST, VLT, Spitzer), submm/mm (ALMA, NOEMA, Herschel, JCMT, IRAM), and radio observations (VLA, MeerKAT, LOFAR). Some selected publications are listed below:
